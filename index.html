The audit always finds the same five leaks

  1. 1
    Payment failures: declines, ACH returns, chargebacks.
  2. 2
    Johnny vs. Susan: the rep who closes ten a month at a 30% default rate is a liability next to the rep who closes fewer and keeps them.
  3. 3
    Marketing that does not match the service: the $25 lead that defaults loses to the $30 lead that sticks. On a $10M portfolio that is $700,000 left on the table.
  4. 4
    The three-day silence. Apprehension spikes after three days without a touch. Cancellations follow.
  5. 5
    Junior case managers on senior work.

The plan, six steps

1
Build the account universe

Every operator in Mint’s six verticals who is not a Mint client, plus the lenders who finance them. Kenton’s client list is the exclusion list.

2
Score them the way Mint would

Volume, seats, CRM, trigger. Triggers: processor termination, chargebacks creeping toward 1.5%, student-loan re-entry, the $30k to $50k plateau, financing declines, hiring.

3
Test every message

On a simulated buyer panel drawn from a library of 8.3 billion personas, cast as the owner of a shop in that vertical, and rewrite until it clears. On the last two books this took reply intent from six of nine readers to nine of nine.

4
Sales intelligence

On every named buyer, so each email matches how that person decides.

5
Mint-branded landing pages

One per vertical, on a site tuned for search and the AI answer engines, built to make the phone ring.

6
The outreach

A drip sequence per person, a short video message where it earns one, a phone day behind it, delivered as a book Mint’s team or ours can run.
